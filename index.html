Research

First author · Springer LNCS 2026

Can an LLM rebuild a published algorithm from the paper alone?

We gave Gemini 2.5 Pro and Claude Sonnet 4 a research paper describing a state-of-the-art Variable Neighborhood Search for balanced clustering. The authors never released their code, and the paper's key update formula is missing a term. We guided both models with a prompting workflow we call CCI (Context, Communication, Iteration) and checked every solution with an independent validator the model never touched.

Gemini's C++ implementation matched the original to within 0.01% and quietly corrected the paper's error. Claude copied the flawed formula, then hid the symptom by periodically recomputing the objective. The result looked plausible, but it was no longer a correct local search.

0.032%
mean gap to the reference implementation
160
paired runs on 16 datasets
p ≈ 2×10−6
Wilcoxon non-inferiority test, 0.01% margin

Why it matters: as LLMs write more research code, the failures that matter most are the ones that still compile and run. An external validator is what caught this one.

Co-author · Preprint, under review

Interpretable ML: models that return equations

Jessamine is an open-source evolutionary symbolic-regression system written in Julia. Instead of fitting a black box, it searches for a prediction function in exact algebraic form. In our preprint it recovers 13 of 14 Strogatz ODE targets and 5 of 8 cp3-bench functions. No algorithm in the original cp3-bench study recovered more than four. We also argue that benchmarks should report a Bayesian reliability rate, meaning the probability that an algorithm recovers the true equation within a fixed compute budget, rather than its best single run.

My role: I ran early design experiments on Jessamine's components: alternative solvers for the parameter layer, and Lasso, Ridge and elastic net for the linear layer (Ridge was kept for speed). Building on my earlier VNS research, I brought a variable-neighborhood mutation schedule into the search, introduced the team to SRBench and the symbolic-regression benchmarking literature our evaluation builds on, and ran benchmark studies (Feynman equations, SRBench/Friedman, Brusselator PDEs). I presented the work at Dynamics Days and SIAM DS 2025.

PhD research · In progress

Stress-testing decision policies for drug-shortage supply chains

Drug shortages hurt patients, and the policies meant to manage them are hard to evaluate. In an agent-based simulation of a multi-echelon pharmaceutical supply chain, I re-examined a deep-RL (actor–critic) ordering policy. I audited its reward design, built a reproducible test harness with pre-registered seed splits, and compared it against simple, transparent rules. The simple heuristics matched the RL agent, and in preliminary studies, routing rules alone removed most of the cost of a disruption.

I also work with real hospital data. I reconciled 4,441 drug-omission records from Dartmouth Health, across 31 purchasing accounts, against FDA shortage data, and found that 73% of the omitted drug molecules were not on the official shortage list.
